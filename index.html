How SUNO would work

Proposed service workflow This is the planned journey. In the current prototype nothing is delivered to a person.

  1. Share a concern

    Describe it in a comfortable way, or just choose a topic.

  2. Choose what you need

    Pick the type of information or support you are looking for.

  3. Explore a pathway

    See an appropriate support pathway and decide your next step.

Why SUNO?

These are intended benefits, not proven outcomes. The research survey is how we plan to test them.

Easier to express

Lower the barrier to putting a concern into words.

Clearer information

Make available support easier to find and understand.

More choice

Let students decide what the next step should be.

More transparency

Be open about privacy and what happens after sharing.

For the ideathon judges

  • Problem: students may avoid asking for help because of fear of judgement, privacy worries or not knowing whom to approach.
  • Journey: Share demonstration shows the proposed flow, with local-only behaviour.
  • Validation: the Student Survey is the research tool. Results appear only from real responses.
  • Safeguards: see Your Trust.
  • Planned next steps: collect survey responses, consult college counsellors, define a safeguarding and response process, then build and test a secure service.

❓ Is it anonymous?

Not asking for a name does not guarantee anonymity. Details you write, or the settings of a survey platform, could still identify someone. SUNO does not claim anonymity.

🌱 How would a future SUNO service work?

Future requirements, not yet implemented

  • Limited, authorised access.
  • A documented data-retention policy.
  • Clear consent and privacy information.
  • Appropriate safeguarding procedures.
  • Secure handling of submissions.
  • A transparent explanation of any limits to confidentiality, for example when someone is at serious risk.

You don’t have to figure everything out alone.

SUNO is not an emergency response system, a diagnostic tool, or a replacement for professional care, and it cannot send help to you. Please confirm contact details on the official websites below before relying on them.

🚨 Urgent safety concerns

If you or someone else may be in immediate danger, call 112 or go to the nearest hospital or a trusted adult. 112 official site

Tele-MANAS 14416 – government mental-health helpline. Official site
CHILDLINE 1098 – for children and young people under 18. Official site

🌱 General wellbeing information

Sleep, movement, breaks and talking to people you trust can all help. WHO mental health overview

🗣️ Speaking to a trusted person

How to start the conversation
  • Pick a calm moment: “Can I talk to you about something?”
  • Start with one sentence: “I’ve been feeling… about…”
  • Write a note if speaking is too hard.
  • Say what you need: to be heard, advice, or help.

🧡 Finding qualified counselling support

Ask whether your college has a counselling or student-support cell. A doctor can also refer you. Tele-MANAS (above) offers trained counsellors.

🛟 College grievance and anti-ragging pathways

Tell a teacher, coordinator or principal you trust, and note what happened, when and who was there. Colleges have grievance and anti-ragging committees. Anti-ragging official site
